What this shows

The Achilles is not one rope. It is three subtendons — from medial gastrocnemius, lateral gastrocnemius and soleus — that spiral around each other on the way to the calcaneus. In this version the muscle–tendon path is modelled the way the tissue is actually arranged: as bundles running longitudinally in parallel, each with its own recruitment threshold and its own territory in the tendon cross-section. Loading the tendon patchily is then not something drawn onto the picture; it is what happens when only some bundles are recruited.

Teaching schematic, not a validated finite-element model. Stresses are multiples of mean tendon stress; centroid offsets are a percentage of tendon radius. No absolute values (MPa, mm, N) are reported, because the model has neither the geometry nor the material properties to justify them.

Why this replaced the proximal/mid/distal zones

Elements in series must carry cumulative force, so in the previous version the distal zone was always the sum and regional detail necessarily disappeared by the free tendon. That is correct for total force but it meant the model structurally could not produce differential sliding between adjacent tendon fascicles — which is the phenomenon actually observed. Non-uniform displacement is a cross-sectional effect between parallel neighbours, not a longitudinal gradient. Parallel bundles generate it natively.

One casualty of the change: the previous version shifted medial gastrocnemius activity proximally with knee flexion, following array EMG findings. That was a longitudinal-region effect and has no representation in a purely parallel model, so it has been removed rather than faked. Knee flexion still reduces gastrocnemius force capacity along its force–length relation and withdraws some drive.

Bundle structure

  • 14 bundles per muscle, 42 in total. Recruitment thresholds are exponentially distributed, as are twitch amplitudes; bundle cross-sectional area scales with twitch amplitude, and areas within a muscle sum to that subtendon's share (SOL 0.50 / MG 0.30 / LG 0.20).
  • Each bundle holds a fixed position inside its subtendon's wedge and carries that position through the twist, so the same bundle can be posterior proximally and deep at the insertion.
  • Bundle stress is its own force divided by its own area, expressed as a multiple of mean tendon stress. Orderly recruitment therefore makes low-intensity contractions spatially patchy, and loading becomes more even as more bundles recruit — an intensity-dependent change in load distribution, not just magnitude.
  • Trial-to-trial noise acts on excitation, so it bites hardest on bundles sitting near their threshold. That is a more defensible locus for the variation than a whole-muscle gain multiplier.

Interfascicular coupling

Perfectly independent strands would overstate non-uniformity, because the interfascicular matrix transmits shear as well as permitting sliding. The coupling parameter κ blends each bundle's stress with a distance-weighted average of its cross-sectional neighbours, using a Gaussian kernel whose width grows with κ (σ = 0.12 + 1.4κ in units of tendon radius). At κ = 0 bundles are independent and non-uniformity is maximal; at κ = 1 the tendon behaves as a bonded unit and stress is near-uniform.

This is the ageing and pathology control. Non-uniform displacement is reduced with ageing, after repair of a ruptured tendon, and with pathological change, so raising κ is the model's way of representing those states. The kernel form and the σ range are modelling choices, not measured values, and κ is not calibrated against any dataset — treat it as a qualitative dial.

The two noise sources

Trial-to-trial fluctuation in synergist activation is partly shared (common descending drive) and partly muscle-specific. Only the second moves the load centroid. Raising common CV alone makes total force vary between trials while the sharing ratio, and therefore the centroid, stays put.

Note on scaling: a given CV on drive emerges as a larger CV on force, because excitation passes through the expansive recruitment-plus-rate-coding nonlinearity. The sliders are drive-level quantities, so the Force spread readout will read higher than the drive CV you set.

Structural assumptions

  • Rotation applied mostly over the distal 5 cm, matching the reported soleus torsion site 3–5 cm proximal to the calcaneal attachment; MG close to parallel in all types.
  • Twist types implemented so the classification falls out of the geometry: Type I only SOL reaches the deep layer of the tuberosity, Type II both LG and SOL, Type III only LG.
  • Bundle positions within each wedge are fixed and arbitrary — there is no established map of which part of a muscle feeds which part of its subtendon.
  • Axial sections use a fixed labelled frame (posterior up, anterior down, medial right, lateral left). Rotation direction is limb-dependent in vivo, so read direction as schematic.
  • Task envelopes are illustrative templates, not measured EMG.
  • "Bundle" here means a sub-element of one muscle and its tendon fascicles. It is deliberately not called a muscle–tendon unit, which conventionally denotes the whole muscle plus its tendon.
