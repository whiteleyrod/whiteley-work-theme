Two independent errors

A hand-held dynamometer is a uniaxial load cell: it registers only the force running along its own long axis. Tilting it costs you on the dial. Where the pad actually sits costs you on the torque. These are separate failures and neither causes the other.

Tilt · second order

The cosine error on the dial

reading = F · cos θ

The limb pushes on the pad perpendicular to the segment. Only the component along the device's axis compresses the load cell; the rest is shear at the pad, carried by skin friction and your hand, and never seen. Because cosine is flat near zero the first few degrees cost almost nothing — 10° of tilt costs 1.5%.

Where device length comes in — and where it does not

θ = asin(Δ / L)

You do not set θ directly; you set where your hand is. A lateral drift Δ between the two ends of the device becomes an angle that depends on L: the same 2 cm of drift is 23.6° on a 5 cm pocket device but 5.7° on a 20 cm one. This is the only route by which device length affects anything. Set θ directly and length drops out entirely.

Placement · first order

The lever-arm error, which has nothing to do with length

torque error = cos θ × d / (d + s) − 1

You put the pad on a mark and measure d to it. If the pad migrates by s during the contraction — skidding on skin, rolling as the limb moves — the true lever arm is d + s while you still divide by d. Swapping a 5 cm device for a 25 cm one and placing the pad on the same mark changes nothing here: the lever arm is a property of where the pad is, not of how long the device is.

This error is first-order in s and does not need any tilt at all. With the device perfectly square and 2 cm of slip on a 35 cm arm you are already 5.4% low on torque with a flawless dial reading.

A note on an earlier version of this model: if you assume the device rotates rigidly about a fixed hand, then s = L·sin θ and length does drive the lever arm. That assumption is not made here, because it inverts the causation — in a real test the pad is placed first and the hand follows it, not the other way round.

The two errors multiply

(1 − 0.034) × (1 − 0.054) = 1 − 0.086

At the opening setup — 15° of tilt, 2 cm of slip, 35 cm arm — the dial is 3.4% low and the placement is 5.4% low, giving 8.6% on the torque estimate. Cosine error is always negative; slip takes its sign from which way the pad went, so a proximal slip can mask the under-read and make the number look better than it is.

Driving it from the keyboard

  • ← → — nudge whichever variable is driving the tilt
  • ↑ ↓ — change the device length
  • [ ] — pad slip; \ puts the pad back on its mark
  • A — swap between driving by angle and by hand drift
  • 0 — back to the opening setup · hold Shift for coarse steps
  • N or ? — this panel; Esc closes it

What the model assumes

  • Frictionless, normal contact. The limb pushes perpendicular to the segment; soft-tissue compliance adds error not shown here.
  • A perfectly uniaxial cell. Real cells have some off-axis and bending sensitivity — device-specific, so check the manufacturer's spec.
  • Rigid segment, joint angle held. d runs from the joint's rotation axis to the centre of the pad.
  • The examiner is not the weak link. These sit on top of the make/break ceiling that dominates testing of strong groups.
