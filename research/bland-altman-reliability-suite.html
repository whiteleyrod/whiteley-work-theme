Why ICC Alone Cannot Establish Agreement (Bland & Altman, 1990)

In Computers in Biology and Medicine (1990; 20:337–340), J. M. Bland and D. G. Altman demonstrated that the intraclass correlation coefficient is intrinsically governed by between-subject sample heterogeneity (the measurement range). A heterogeneous cohort can yield ICC > 0.90 despite clinically unacceptable error, whereas a homogeneous cohort yields low ICC even when differences are trivial. Furthermore, ICC conflates systematic bias with random error and ignores clinical tolerance bounds. Always report 95% Limits of Agreement alongside ICC.

Jones (2019) — Journal of Biopharmaceutical Statistics

Arithmetic Error in Sum of Squared Replicates (∑mᵢ²)

In Bland & Altman (2007, p. 576), the authors reported ∑mᵢ² = 312 for the 12 cardiac ejection fraction subjects with replicates mᵢ = [5, 4, 6, 5, 5, 4, 4, 7, 3, 5, 6, 6]. William Stephen Jones (2019) proved the true sum is:

5² + 4² + 6² + 5² + 5² + 4² + 4² + 7² + 3² + 5² + 6² + 6² = 314

Divisor m₀ = ((60² − ∑mᵢ²) / ((12−1)·60)) shifts from 4.981818 (with 312) to 4.978788 (with 314).

Matsubayashi (2021) — Journal of Biopharmaceutical Statistics

Table 1 Transcription Displacement vs B&A (1999) Dataset

Jun Matsubayashi (2021) discovered that Table 1 of Bland & Altman (2007, p. 572) misprinted subject ID 6 instead of 5 in row 6 and displaced rows 6–14 relative to Table 4 of Bland & Altman (1999, p. 154). Consequently:

Printed 2007 Table 1 ANOVA: MS_b = 3.960641, MS_w = 0.227649 (df=48) B&A (1999) True Data ANOVA: MS_b = 4.209086, MS_w = 0.170714 (df=48)

Bland & Altman's 2007 text calculations matched the uncorrupted 1999 dataset (where ∑mᵢ² actually was 312). Switch between the 1999 and 2007 Cardiac Output presets above to inspect both live.
