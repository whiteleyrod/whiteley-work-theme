Tendon micromechanics

A tendon lengthens mostly by sliding, not by stretching.

Load the tendon and watch the fiducial lines. At rest they run straight across each bundle. Under load they break into a staircase — at every level of the hierarchy — because neighbouring units shear past one another through the matrix between them.

Where you are on the curve

Teaching notes

Why the staircase matters

If a tendon extended purely by stretching its collagen, fibril strain would equal tissue strain and fibrils would rupture well inside the physiological range. Sliding is the protective mechanism — it lets the tissue lengthen while keeping fibril-level strain low.

Energy-storing vs positional

Energy-storing tendons (Achilles, equine SDFT) have a more extensible, more fatigue-resistant interfascicular matrix and rely heavily on interfascicular sliding. Positional tendons transmit strain more directly to the fascicles. Switch the toggle and compare the ladder.

What ageing changes

The interfascicular matrix stiffens and loses sliding capacity with age and with non-enzymatic cross-linking. Extension previously absorbed by shear now has to be taken up as material strain. The same joint excursion delivers higher fibril strain.

Using this in the clinic

It reframes tendon loading: rehabilitation is not only about fibril tensile capacity, but about the shear behaviour of the matrix between bundles. It also explains why fatigue loading damages the matrix before it damages the collagen.

Underlying evidence

  1. Screen HRC, Lee DA, Bader DL, Shelton JC. An investigation into the effects of the hierarchical structure of tendon fascicles on micromechanical properties. Proc Inst Mech Eng H. 2004;218(2):109–19. — fibre sliding dominates fascicle extension.
  2. Thorpe CT, Udeze CP, Birch HL, Clegg PD, Screen HRC. Specialization of tendon mechanical properties results from interfascicular differences. J R Soc Interface. 2012;9(76):3108–17. — the interfascicular matrix is more extensible in energy-storing tendon.
  3. Thorpe CT, Riley GP, Birch HL, Clegg PD, Screen HRC. Fascicles and the interfascicular matrix show adaptation to fatigue loading. Acta Biomater. 2016;42:308–15. — matrix, not fascicle, is the fatigue-sensitive compartment.
  4. Fratzl P, Misof K, Zizak I, et al. Fibrillar structure and mechanical properties of collagen. J Struct Biol. 1998;122(1–2):119–22. — fibril strain is a fraction of tissue strain.
  5. Sasaki N, Odajima S. Elongation mechanism of collagen fibrils and force–strain relations of tendon at each level of structural hierarchy. J Biomech. 1996;29(9):1131–6. — molecular strain roughly a tenth of tissue strain.
  6. Screen HRC, Berk DE, Kadler KE, Ramirez F, Young MF. Tendon functional extracellular matrix. J Orthop Res. 2015;33(6):793–9. — review of matrix roles in load transfer.
